# Teacher quick-start (10 minutes)

## 1. Open it

There is nothing to install and no sign-up. It runs in any modern browser, including the low-cost Android phones many learners have. Progress is stored on each learner's own device.

## 2. Project a simulation in class

Open **Toolkit** and filter by grade or strand. Each simulation waits for you to press **Play**, so you can ask the class to **predict first**:

- **Motion lab**: set the initial velocity and acceleration. The class sketches the x-t, v-t and a-t graphs, then you press Play.
- **Energy skate park**: switch friction on and off and watch kinetic, potential and mechanical energy as bars.
- **Forces on a slope** and **Work & energy with friction**: free-body diagrams that update live. Ideal for Newton's second law and the work-energy theorem.
- **Collision lab**: elastic or inelastic? Check whether the momentum and kinetic-energy totals stay the same.
- **Projectile lab**: compare the displacement, velocity and acceleration graphs of a ball thrown upwards.
- **Circuit lab**: add a resistor in parallel. Does the ammeter reading go up or down? Switch on internal resistance for Grade 12.
- **Refraction & TIR**: raise the angle of incidence past the critical angle.
- **Photoelectric effect**: change the intensity and then the frequency. Only one of them changes the kinetic energy of the electrons.
- **Periodic table & trends**, **Build an atom** and **Molecular shapes**: the chemistry warm-ups.
- **Le Chatelier's principle**: add a reactant, raise the temperature or squeeze the container, and watch the concentrations re-settle.
- **Virtual titration**: add one drop at a time, watch the indicator change colour, and read the equivalence point on the pH curve.
- **Galvanic & electrolytic cells**: pick two metals, read the emf, and watch the electrons flow from anode to cathode.

## 3. Set a lesson as homework

Every lesson has a link like `/learn/newtons-laws`. Learners see their progress on each lesson and on the Learn page. Each lesson ends with a quiz, and many include an exam-style question with a memo.

## 4. Use the Exam Room

Learners do the working first, then open the memo and tick each mark they earned, exactly the way NSC markers work. Use it as self-marked homework or as a revision station.

## 4b. Write a mock paper

**Exam Room → Mock papers** has a Paper 1 (Physics) and Paper 2 (Chemistry) mock for each grade. There are also quick mocks of about 50 marks, with a new mix every time. Run a mock in class with the timer, or print the paper and memo from the start screen. Grade 12 Paper 1 includes Newton's laws and electrostatics from Grade 11, as the NSC paper does.

## 4c. Print a worksheet

**Teachers → Worksheet generator** makes a printable worksheet from any mix of topics, with answer space and a full memo on a separate page. The same link always gives the same questions. Change the worksheet number to get a fresh set.

## 5. Use the drills for differentiation

The **Endless drill** in each lesson (and in the Toolkit) generates new questions forever. The strongest learners can chase streaks while you work with a small group. The **Weak-spot coach** on each learner's Progress page names the three topics to work on next.

## 6. Accessibility in one click

The **Preferences** button (top right) gives each learner:
- dyslexia-friendly fonts
- bigger text
- calm mode (no animation)
- a reading ruler
- word anchors
- read-aloud speed
- focus mode

Show your class where it is on day one.

## 7. Safety first

Lessons with practical work include **Lab safety** callouts: handling acids, heating, gas tests and electricity. The simulations support practical work; they don't replace it. Do the prescribed practical activities and formal assessments as set out in CAPS.
