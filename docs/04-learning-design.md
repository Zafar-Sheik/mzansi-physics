# Learning design & study tools

Science is a *doing* subject. Every lesson is built so learners spend most of their time predicting, experimenting and calculating, not just reading.

## The six-step lesson

1. **Hook.** A "Mzansi moment": a taxi pulling off, Gautrain braking, a braai gas bottle, load-shedding and batteries, Sasol, gold mining.
2. **See it.** A live simulation. The learner predicts, presses Play, and reads the *Explain* box.
3. **Do it.** Worked examples revealed **one step at a time**, each with a "Why?". Then typed-answer practice with hints and a full solution.
4. **Check it.** A quick quiz with an explanation for every answer.
5. **Exam it.** An NSC-style question with a memo.
6. **Celebrate.** XP, streaks and badges.

## Why these tools?

- **Predict-Observe-Explain.** Simulations never start on their own. Committing to a prediction before pressing Play exposes misconceptions such as "heavier things fall faster" or "current gets used up", and the *Explain* box resolves them.
- **Worked examples and self-explanation.** Learners new to a method learn more from studying worked examples than from solving problems cold.
- **Retrieval practice.** Quizzes, flashcards and typed practice make learners pull information out of memory.
- **Immediate, specific feedback.** The checker spots sign errors (direction), unit-prefix slips (cm vs m, kJ vs J), factor-of-2 slips and forgotten g.
- **Definitions matter.** NSC papers award marks for exact definitions and laws. Every one is a flashcard, and Science Hangman and True/False Blitz make learning them a game.
- **Interleaving.** The Quiz Arena and drills mix topics.
- **Spaced repetition.** Flashcards use Leitner boxes.
- **Dual coding.** Every formula is paired with a picture: graphs, vector arrows, particle models, energy bars and field lines.

## Typed answers

Learners type answers the way they would write them:
- quantities with units: `9,8 m·s⁻²`, `12 m/s`, `2 kJ`
- scientific notation: `3 × 10^8`, `3e8`, `6,63 × 10⁻³⁴`
- equations: `v = 4`
- chemical formulae: `H2SO4`
- names: `2-methylbutane`
- lists: `2; 7; 4; 6`

Decimal commas and points both work. A keypad gives phone users quick access to ×10ⁿ, ⁻, ·, Ω, μ and subscripts.

## Motivation without pressure

XP rewards *effort*, such as reading a step, attempting a problem or running a simulation, not just correct answers. A wrong answer always gets "Not yet". Streaks and badges are visible but never punish anyone.
